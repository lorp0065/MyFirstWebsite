Opossum Habitat

From deep woods to city backyards — opossums live closer than you think.

Facts Habitat

Virginia opossums are remarkably adaptable. They thrive in many environments across North America and have become a familiar sight in suburban and urban neighborhoods. Here's where you're most likely to find them.

  • Range across North America Virginia opossums live from southern Canada through the United States and into Mexico and Central America. They are the only marsupial native to countries north of Mexico.
  • Forests and woodlands Opossums prefer areas with plenty of cover — deciduous and mixed forests, wooded stream banks, and brushy fields. Trees provide climbing routes and safe places to rest during the day.
  • Suburbs and cities As natural habitat shrinks, opossums have moved into neighborhoods. They forage in gardens, alleys, and parks, taking advantage of pet food, compost, fruit trees, and the insects that gather near homes.
  • Den sites and shelter During the day, opossums sleep in hollow logs, tree cavities, rock crevices, and abandoned burrows dug by other animals. In urban areas they may use crawl spaces, sheds, or brush piles — anywhere dark and quiet.
  • Near water Wetlands, marshes, and stream edges are common opossum territory. These areas offer drinking water and attract the insects, frogs, and small animals that opossums eat.
  • Seasonal movement Opossums don't hibernate, but they may shift their range as seasons change, following food sources. In colder months they stay closer to reliable shelter and whatever food they can find.
