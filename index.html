Opossum Facts

North America's only native marsupial — more interesting than you might think.

Facts Habitat

Opossums are often misunderstood backyard visitors. They're shy, helpful pest controllers, and full of surprising adaptations. Here are a few things worth knowing about them.

  • North America's only marsupial Like kangaroos, female opossums carry their young in a pouch. Babies, called joeys, stay there until they're old enough to ride on their mother's back.
  • They "play dead" When threatened, an opossum may collapse, go stiff, and drool — a defense called thanatosis. Predators often lose interest in what looks like a dead animal.
  • Natural tick control Opossums groom constantly and eat ticks they find on themselves. A single opossum can consume thousands of ticks in one season, helping reduce tick populations near homes.
  • More teeth than any North American mammal Opossums have 50 teeth — more than any other mammal on the continent. They use them to eat fruit, insects, small animals, and even carrion.
  • Immune to most snake venom Opossums produce a protein that neutralizes venom from rattlesnakes and other pit vipers, letting them eat snakes that would be deadly to most animals.
  • Prehensile tail and opposable thumbs Their hairless tail can grip branches for balance, and their hind feet have opposable thumbs — useful for climbing and grasping food.
  • Mostly nocturnal and solitary Opossums come out at night to forage alone. They're generally non-aggressive and prefer to avoid confrontation rather than fight.
